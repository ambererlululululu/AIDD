## Pharmacophore and Binding Mode

C34 adopts a non-covalent reversible binding mode in the ATP pocket:

### Position-by-Position Analysis:
1. **Pyrimidine** (hinge binder): H-bonds with hinge region residues (MET793 critical). DO NOT MODIFY.
2. **Thiazole** (central linker): Connects hinge binder to hydrophobic region. DO NOT MODIFY.
3. **4-Fluorophenyl** (Aryl position → hydrophobic pocket): Van der Waals with gatekeeper (MET790). MODIFIABLE.
4. **Ethylsulfonyl-piperidine** (Sulfonyl position → solvent): PK/solubility handle. MODIFIABLE.
5. **Indole-dimethylaminoethyl** (Tail → solvent-exposed): Modulates PK, brain penetration. MODIFIABLE.

## SAR Findings by Position

### Aryl Position (para-F phenyl)
- **para-F optimal**: Electron withdrawal enhances hinge interaction via thiazole
- **para-Cl, para-Br**: Slightly reduced potency (steric clash with MET790 pocket)
- **para-CH₃**: Tolerated but LogP increase without binding benefit
- **para-OMe, para-NH₂**: Strongly electron-donating → reduced activity
- **para-CF₃**: Maintains potency but MW/LogP penalty

### Sulfonyl Position (EtSO₂-piperidine)
- **Sulfonyl critical for solubility**: Reduces LogP vs alkyl chains
- **Et optimal**: MeSO₂ too small (less metabolic shielding), PrSO₂ diminishing returns + MW
- **Cyclic sultam**: Improved metabolic stability but synthetic difficulty
- **Carboxamide replacement**: Significantly reduced solubility

### Tail Position (indole + NMe₂-ethyl)
- **NMe₂-ethyl basicity essential**: pKa ~8-9 for aqueous solubility
- **Removing basic amine**: Dramatically reduced oral bioavailability
- **Morpholine replacement**: Tolerated, may improve metabolic stability
- **Propyl linker**: Reduced potency vs ethyl
- **Indole N-methylation**: Tolerated without potency loss

## Design Constraints
1. MW 634 — near upper limit. Prefer MW-neutral modifications
2. Measured LogP ~6.2 — above Ro5, reducing LogP is priority
3. Must maintain non-covalent mechanism (no electrophilic warheads)
4. F=30.72% acceptable but improvable via LogP/TPSA optimization
5. WT EGFR selectivity critical — avoid increasing WT binding
